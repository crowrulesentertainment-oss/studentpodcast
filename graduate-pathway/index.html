Your Next Chapter: Student to Creator

From classroom projects to future creative opportunities.

Students who complete the Academy and want to continue creating may choose to explore CrowRules Podcasting through a separate application and onboarding process. This is optional, age-appropriate, and never a condition of Academy participation.

Academy homeFor schoolsSupport the AcademyStudent-to-Creator

01 · Stage 1 · Learn

Participate in the school-sponsored Academy, complete learning modules, and create educator-reviewed projects.

Core educational access remains free.

02 · Stage 2 · Complete

Receive Academy completion recognition, pathway achievements, and a portfolio of work the student is authorized to retain or share.

The school controls academic recognition and records.

03 · Stage 3 · Express interest

Eligible graduates may choose to learn about creator opportunities or submit an interest application.

No automatic account transfer, no pressure, and no guarantee of acceptance or paid work.

04 · Stage 4 · Separate onboarding

Review age eligibility, guardian permissions where needed, creator standards, copyright, ownership, publishing permissions, and role expectations.

Student academic records are not transferred automatically.

05 · Stage 5 · Explore creator roles

Potential roles include host, co-host, producer, audio editor, video producer, journalist, scriptwriter, designer, production assistant, and show developer.

Any opportunity is subject to availability, separate approval, and applicable age and work rules.

06 · Age-appropriate safeguards

Under-13 students remain within the school-sponsored Academy experience. Ages 13–17 require a separately reviewed youth pathway with guardian consent and oversight. Adults may apply through standard creator onboarding.

Final age rules must be checked against applicable law, platform terms, and CrowRules policy before activation.

Always free for schools and students.

The Academy's educational experience is designed to remain free. CrowRules sustains the program through organizational support, sponsorships, funded partnerships, and separate commercial services. Student privacy, school oversight, and educational purpose remain central.
