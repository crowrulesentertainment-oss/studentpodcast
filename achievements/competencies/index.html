Achievement Levels & Skill Map

A developmental progression and competency map across six media learning pathways.

An educator-facing framework for recognizing learning and documenting growth. Schools retain student records and determine all academic outcomes.

Achievement overviewSkill mapPortfolio guideCertificates

Four developmental levels

LEVEL 01

Explorer

Recognizes media forms, audience, purpose, basic roles, and responsible participation. Completes structured tasks with guidance.

Typical evidence: media analysis, guided research notes, idea pitch, reflection.

LEVEL 02

Creator

Plans and develops a focused project, uses supported sources, drafts content, and applies basic production and ethical practices.

Typical evidence: outline or storyboard, source list, draft, peer/educator feedback.

LEVEL 03

Producer

Coordinates a more independent project, makes intentional creative choices, revises from feedback, and documents team contributions.

Typical evidence: production plan, revised artifact, quality checklist, contribution log.

LEVEL 04

Advanced Producer

Integrates research, craft, ethics, accessibility, collaboration, and reflection in a substantial capstone or sustained body of work.

Typical evidence: capstone package, documented revisions, reflection, educator assessment.

These labels are learning descriptors only. They are not automatically assigned by age or grade, do not represent a nationally recognized qualification, and do not imply mastery of every skill in a level.

Eight shared module competencies

  1. Introduction to Media — identify format, audience, purpose, and production roles.
  2. Research and Fact-Checking — assess sources, corroborate claims, and cite evidence.
  3. Writing and Storytelling — organize a clear message for a chosen medium.
  4. Public Speaking and Communication — communicate clearly, listen actively, and interview respectfully.
  5. Audio and Video Production — plan sound, visuals, sequencing, and accessible alternatives.
  6. Media Ethics and Responsibility — apply privacy, consent, copyright, fairness, and correction practices.
  7. Teamwork and Project Management — define roles, milestones, and feedback practices.
  8. Final Production and Portfolio — integrate skills, revise, and reflect on learning.

Suggested evidence standard

Recognition should be based on observable evidence and stated criteria, not popularity, expensive equipment, public exposure, personality, or access to recording technology. Educators should provide equivalent non-recording and accessible ways to demonstrate each outcome.
