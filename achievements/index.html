Recognize growth—not just the finished product

The framework gives educators a consistent way to describe developing media skills across projects and pathways. It is flexible by design: a student may demonstrate a skill through writing, a storyboard, an accessible presentation, a team role, or a school-approved media artifact. Recording is never required to demonstrate learning.

How recognition works

  1. Educator selects relevant learning outcomes and explains expectations.
  2. Student gathers evidence through the assigned project and accessible alternatives.
  3. Educator reviews evidence using school-selected criteria and gives feedback.
  4. Student revises and reflects where appropriate.
  5. School decides grades and academic credit; Academy recognition may document participation or demonstrated skills.

Private by default

Portfolios stay with the school in school-approved systems. This public website does not create student accounts, collect student names, host portfolio files, or publish student work. Sharing work publicly requires separate school approval and all applicable permissions.

Important academic distinction

CrowRules achievement recognition is non-credit-bearing recognition from the Academy. It is not an accredited credential and does not award grades, course credit, standards alignment, or transcript recognition. Only the school or district may make those decisions.
