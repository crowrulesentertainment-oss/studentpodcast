Introducing CrowRules Student Media Academy—a free, school-sponsored media education program for middle and high school students.

This proposal invites school districts to explore a supervised pilot that uses podcasting, journalism, film, writing, design, and production projects to reinforce communication, research, collaboration, digital citizenship, and media literacy.

The proposal in one sentence

Give students an educator-guided opportunity to learn by creating media—without charging students or schools for access to the Academy's core educational program.

01 · The opportunity

Students learn communication, research, writing, public speaking, critical thinking, teamwork, and digital production by planning and producing meaningful media projects.

Designed for middle school and high school contexts.

02 · The program

Six learning pathways: podcasting and broadcasting; journalism and newsroom; film and video; writing and storytelling; digital media and design; media leadership and production.

A shared learning framework with adaptable projects.

03 · Academic flexibility

Districts and educators may evaluate projects for classroom assignments, enrichment, extra credit, independent study, or future elective and credit-pathway review.

Only the district or school determines grades, standards alignment, and credit.

04 · No participation fee

The Academy's core educational experience is intended to remain free to participating schools and students. CrowRules seeks external sponsorships, funded partnerships, and separate commercial revenue to sustain operations.

No required student purchases, subscriptions, or sponsor access.

Suggested six-week pilot

Week 1 · Discover

Media formats, audience, project choices, team roles, and learning goals.

Week 2 · Research

Research questions, source quality, evidence, citations, and fact-checking.

Week 3 · Write

Script, outline, interview questions, storyboard, and teacher review.

Week 4 · Produce

Recording, presentation, production roles, and private draft creation.

Week 5 · Revise

Feedback, accuracy, editing, accessibility, and improvement.

Week 6 · Reflect

Final project, student reflection, educator assessment, and completion recognition. Public release only if separately approved.

Roles and responsibilities

District / school

Determine program fit; authorize participation; designate staff; control enrollment; review curriculum and technology; establish grading and credit decisions; approve publishing; and set required student/guardian permissions.

CrowRules Entertainment

Provide the Academy framework, learning resources, project ideas, educator planning support, program recognition, and agreed technology services; maintain operational safeguards and report aggregate program impact.

Safety and procurement review

Before any student launch

Review data collection, student identity, account provisioning, storage, access controls, retention/deletion, accessibility, incident response, vendor agreements, and applicable student privacy requirements.

Publishing controls

Student work stays private by default. Public release requires educator review, school authorization, platform moderation, and all required permissions. Sponsors receive no student data or access.

What we are asking the district to consider

Nominate an interested school or educator team for a planning conversation; identify a suitable grade band and learning context; review the proposed pilot and safeguards; and determine whether a small, supervised pilot is appropriate. This is an invitation to explore—not a claim of district endorsement, procurement approval, or an existing partnership.
