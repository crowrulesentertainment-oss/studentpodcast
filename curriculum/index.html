02 · Thirty initial assignments

English and writing · History and social studies · Science · Mathematics · Journalism and communication · Technology and media arts.

Examples: historical event explainer, science explainer, community interview, student newsroom, budgeting podcast, data story, documentary, and original audio story.

03 · Assessment framework

A suggested 100-point rubric: research and accuracy 25; writing and organization 20; communication 15; production quality 15; creativity 10; reflection 10; professionalism 5.

Educators can adapt criteria and determine whether work earns grades, extra credit, enrichment, or independent-study recognition.

04 · Student project workflow

Explore a topic, research and cite sources, create an outline or script, produce the project, submit privately, receive educator feedback, revise, and reflect.

The program tracks learning evidence; schools retain authority over academic grading and credit decisions.

Always free for schools and students.

The Academy's educational experience is designed to remain free. CrowRules sustains the program through organizational support, sponsorships, funded partnerships, and separate commercial services. Student privacy, school oversight, and educational purpose remain central.
