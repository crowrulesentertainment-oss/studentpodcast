CURRICULUM LIBRARY · MODULE 02

Research and Fact-Checking

Develop a focused question, evaluate sources, corroborate claims, and document evidence accurately.

All modulesEducator toolkitSafety framework

At a glance

Suggested time: 75–90 minutes

Audience: Middle school and high school; adapt complexity and pacing.

Materials: Educator-selected examples, paper or digital planning tools approved by the school, and optional school-approved recording equipment.

Learning objective

Develop a focused question, evaluate sources, corroborate claims, and document evidence accurately.

Key vocabulary: primary source; secondary source; corroboration; bias; claim; citation

Educator preparation

Select age-appropriate examples, preview all media, identify accommodations, and decide what evidence students will create. Confirm that any device, software, storage, or classroom sharing method is approved by the school. Educators may map this lesson to their own course and district outcomes; no formal standards alignment is claimed here.

Teaching sequence

  1. Launch: Explain the learning objective, vocabulary, and project expectations.
  2. Model: Demonstrate the core skill using a teacher-selected example.
  3. Guided practice: Work through a short example together; ask students to explain their choices.
  4. Independent or team practice: Turn a broad topic into a focused research question. Model lateral reading and checking author, date, evidence, and purpose. Compare at least two independent sources. Separate verifiable facts, interpretations, and opinions; record what remains uncertain.
  5. Review and revise: Invite students to use the checklist and educator feedback to improve their work.

Student assignment

What students create

Submit a research question, annotated source list with at least three sources (educator may adjust), claim-evidence notes, and a short fact-check explaining one claim.

Knowledge check and assessment evidence

Knowledge check: source credibility and corroboration scenarios. Evidence: source notes, citations, and a corrected or qualified claim.

Reflection prompts

What would change your confidence in a claim? Why is repeating a claim not the same as verifying it?

Differentiation and accessibility

Provide curated source sets, reading-level alternatives, vocabulary previews, and audio access where available.

Safety and publishing

Do not investigate private individuals or collect sensitive personal information. Avoid contacting sources without educator approval. Student work remains private by default. Do not use this informational website to collect student work or personal information.

Educator authority

This is a suggested instructional resource, not a prescribed course or official standards certification. Teachers and districts determine grading, accommodations, curriculum alignment, credit, and whether any work may be shared. CrowRules Academy recognition does not award school credit.
