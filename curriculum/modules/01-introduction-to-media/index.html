CURRICULUM LIBRARY · MODULE 01

Introduction to Media

Recognize common media formats and explain how purpose, audience, and format shape a message.

All modulesEducator toolkitSafety framework

At a glance

Suggested time: 60–75 minutes

Audience: Middle school and high school; adapt complexity and pacing.

Materials: Educator-selected examples, paper or digital planning tools approved by the school, and optional school-approved recording equipment.

Learning objective

Recognize common media formats and explain how purpose, audience, and format shape a message.

Key vocabulary: media literacy; audience; purpose; format; producer

Educator preparation

Select age-appropriate examples, preview all media, identify accommodations, and decide what evidence students will create. Confirm that any device, software, storage, or classroom sharing method is approved by the school. Educators may map this lesson to their own course and district outcomes; no formal standards alignment is claimed here.

Teaching sequence

  1. Launch: Explain the learning objective, vocabulary, and project expectations.
  2. Model: Demonstrate the core skill using a teacher-selected example.
  3. Guided practice: Work through a short example together; ask students to explain their choices.
  4. Independent or team practice: Analyze two contrasting media examples. Identify who created each, intended audience, purpose, evidence, and techniques. Compare a podcast, news report, documentary, interview, and explainer. In teams, pitch a short school-appropriate media project and explain why its format fits its audience.
  5. Review and revise: Invite students to use the checklist and educator feedback to improve their work.

Student assignment

What students create

Create a one-page media analysis and a project pitch (working title, audience, purpose, format, roles, and key question).

Knowledge check and assessment evidence

Exit ticket: identify purpose, audience, and one production choice in a sample. Project evidence: analysis and pitch.

Reflection prompts

What makes a media message trustworthy? How does the format change what an audience experiences?

Differentiation and accessibility

Use captioned examples, transcripts, visual organizers, and choice of written, spoken, or drawn responses.

Safety and publishing

Do not record classmates or publish work. Any example involving real people requires educator review and appropriate permission. Student work remains private by default. Do not use this informational website to collect student work or personal information.

Educator authority

This is a suggested instructional resource, not a prescribed course or official standards certification. Teachers and districts determine grading, accommodations, curriculum alignment, credit, and whether any work may be shared. CrowRules Academy recognition does not award school credit.
