CURRICULUM LIBRARY · MODULE 05

Audio and Video Production

Plan and create a basic media artifact using intentional recording, framing, sound, editing, and accessibility choices.

All modulesEducator toolkitSafety framework

At a glance

Suggested time: 90–120 minutes

Audience: Middle school and high school; adapt complexity and pacing.

Materials: Educator-selected examples, paper or digital planning tools approved by the school, and optional school-approved recording equipment.

Learning objective

Plan and create a basic media artifact using intentional recording, framing, sound, editing, and accessibility choices.

Key vocabulary: shot; frame; microphone; ambient sound; edit; caption; accessibility

Educator preparation

Select age-appropriate examples, preview all media, identify accommodations, and decide what evidence students will create. Confirm that any device, software, storage, or classroom sharing method is approved by the school. Educators may map this lesson to their own course and district outcomes; no formal standards alignment is claimed here.

Teaching sequence

  1. Launch: Explain the learning objective, vocabulary, and project expectations.
  2. Model: Demonstrate the core skill using a teacher-selected example.
  3. Guided practice: Work through a short example together; ask students to explain their choices.
  4. Independent or team practice: Compare clear and unclear recordings. Demonstrate microphone distance, quiet recording spaces, stable framing, simple lighting, and basic editing concepts. Plan a short artifact, capture a supervised practice sample, review quality, and revise one technical element.
  5. Review and revise: Invite students to use the checklist and educator feedback to improve their work.

Student assignment

What students create

Submit a production plan and a short practice artifact or storyboard demonstrating deliberate sound or visual choices. A non-recorded mock-up is an acceptable alternative.

Knowledge check and assessment evidence

Use a quality checklist for intelligibility, framing, sound, and accessibility. Evidence: plan, draft, revision note.

Reflection prompts

Which production choice most improves audience understanding? What can captions or transcripts make accessible?

Differentiation and accessibility

Offer paper storyboards, accessible editing tools approved by the school, partner roles, and non-recording alternatives.

Safety and publishing

Use school-approved devices and storage only. No public upload, personal account creation, or third-party tool use without school review. Student work remains private by default. Do not use this informational website to collect student work or personal information.

Educator authority

This is a suggested instructional resource, not a prescribed course or official standards certification. Teachers and districts determine grading, accommodations, curriculum alignment, credit, and whether any work may be shared. CrowRules Academy recognition does not award school credit.
