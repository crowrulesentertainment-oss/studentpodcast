CURRICULUM LIBRARY · MODULE 04

Public Speaking and Communication

Use audience-aware speaking, active listening, respectful interviewing, and clear presentation techniques.

All modulesEducator toolkitSafety framework

At a glance

Suggested time: 60–75 minutes

Audience: Middle school and high school; adapt complexity and pacing.

Materials: Educator-selected examples, paper or digital planning tools approved by the school, and optional school-approved recording equipment.

Learning objective

Use audience-aware speaking, active listening, respectful interviewing, and clear presentation techniques.

Key vocabulary: active listening; open-ended question; pacing; tone; follow-up; paraphrase

Educator preparation

Select age-appropriate examples, preview all media, identify accommodations, and decide what evidence students will create. Confirm that any device, software, storage, or classroom sharing method is approved by the school. Educators may map this lesson to their own course and district outcomes; no formal standards alignment is claimed here.

Teaching sequence

  1. Launch: Explain the learning objective, vocabulary, and project expectations.
  2. Model: Demonstrate the core skill using a teacher-selected example.
  3. Guided practice: Work through a short example together; ask students to explain their choices.
  4. Independent or team practice: Demonstrate clear delivery and active listening. Compare open and closed questions. Students prepare neutral questions, practice introductions and follow-ups, then deliver a short presentation or conduct a role-play interview. Give feedback on clarity, pacing, and respectful listening.
  5. Review and revise: Invite students to use the checklist and educator feedback to improve their work.

Student assignment

What students create

Present a 1–2 minute talk or complete a paired mock interview and submit a brief communication self-assessment.

Knowledge check and assessment evidence

Observe a practice round with a communication checklist. Evidence: question plan, presentation or role-play, and reflection.

Reflection prompts

How can a speaker make information understandable? How does a good follow-up question improve understanding?

Differentiation and accessibility

Allow rehearsal, cue cards, small-group presentation, recorded audio where authorized, or written interview alternatives.

Safety and publishing

No real interviews, recording, or sharing without educator authorization and required consent. Students may pass on personal disclosure. Student work remains private by default. Do not use this informational website to collect student work or personal information.

Educator authority

This is a suggested instructional resource, not a prescribed course or official standards certification. Teachers and districts determine grading, accommodations, curriculum alignment, credit, and whether any work may be shared. CrowRules Academy recognition does not award school credit.
