How to use this toolkit

Choose a learning objective, select a project, define the evidence students will produce, and decide how you will assess it. The Academy provides a framework—not a substitute for your curriculum, district standards, or professional judgment.

01 · Introduction to Media

Compare podcasts, news reports, documentaries, interviews, and explainers. Identify audience, purpose, format, and production roles.

Evidence: media analysis and a project pitch.

02 · Research & Fact-Checking

Generate research questions, evaluate source credibility, corroborate claims, distinguish fact from opinion, and cite sources.

Evidence: annotated source list and fact-check notes.

03 · Writing & Storytelling

Build a clear beginning, middle, and end; write for the ear or screen; develop interview questions and transitions.

Evidence: outline, script, or storyboard.

04 · Public Speaking & Communication

Practice voice, pacing, active listening, interview etiquette, audience awareness, and clear presentation.

Evidence: recorded practice or presentation reflection.

05 · Audio & Video Production

Explore microphones, framing, lighting, recording environments, editing, sound levels, and accessible presentation.

Evidence: production plan and draft media artifact.

06 · Media Ethics & Responsibility

Discuss accuracy, bias, privacy, consent, copyright, representation, corrections, and responsible publishing.

Evidence: ethical review checklist.

07 · Teamwork & Project Management

Assign roles, create schedules, track tasks, give constructive feedback, and resolve production challenges.

Evidence: team plan and contribution reflection.

08 · Final Production & Portfolio

Combine research, writing, communication, and production into a final project; revise and explain what was learned.

Evidence: final project, sources, reflection, and teacher assessment.

Thirty assignment starters

English & Writing

1. Turn a short story into an audio scene. 2. Produce a book discussion episode. 3. Write and record a persuasive audio essay. 4. Create a character interview. 5. Compare two storytelling techniques.

Skills: composition, voice, structure, argument, revision.

History & Social Studies

6. Create a historical event explainer. 7. Build a timeline narration. 8. Compare primary and secondary sources. 9. Produce a local-history interview plan. 10. Explain a civic process.

Skills: evidence, chronology, context, source evaluation.

Science

11. Record a science concept explainer. 12. Make a myth-versus-evidence segment. 13. Explain a local ecosystem. 14. Interview a scientist using prepared questions. 15. Create a lab-process audio guide.

Skills: explanation, evidence, vocabulary, scientific reasoning.

Mathematics

16. Explain a real-world math problem. 17. Build a budget for a mock production. 18. Tell a data story using a chart. 19. Compare statistics in two reports. 20. Explain probability through an everyday example.

Skills: quantitative reasoning, interpretation, precision.

Journalism & Communication

21. Produce a school-safe news brief. 22. Create a balanced interview. 23. Fact-check a public claim. 24. Write a community feature. 25. Compare headline framing.

Skills: reporting, fairness, interviewing, media literacy.

Technology & Media Arts

26. Design a show identity kit. 27. Create a storyboard. 28. Produce a soundscape. 29. Make a short instructional video plan. 30. Document a team production workflow.

Skills: design, technical fluency, creativity, collaboration.

Grade-band adaptation

Middle school · Explore and practice

Shorter projects, structured templates, small teams, guided source lists, clear role cards, and frequent teacher check-ins. Focus on foundational communication, digital citizenship, and creative confidence.

High school · Research and produce

Longer projects, deeper source evaluation, student-led planning, specialized production roles, revision cycles, and portfolio reflection. Connect work to course outcomes and career exploration.

Academic recognition belongs to the school.

Teachers may choose to use activities for classroom assignments, extra credit, enrichment, or approved independent study. Any formal credit, grades, standards alignment, and transcript decisions must be made by the school or district. CrowRules certificates document Academy participation and achievement only.
